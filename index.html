The project

WHAT IS THIS PROJECT?

This project is a roof-mounted misting system developed for an outdoor campus waiting area at the Polytechnic University of the Philippines – Institute of Technology.

The system monitors environmental conditions such as temperature, relative humidity, and wind velocity. Based on predetermined conditions, the system can automatically operate the misting system and record the resulting environmental data.

The reason

WHY DOES IT MATTER?

Outdoor campus spaces can become uncomfortable during hot weather. This thesis project is intended to monitor thermal conditions, provide a controlled misting response, and collect data so the system can be evaluated. It does not claim to solve heat discomfort. It is an experiment, and the data below is part of that experiment.

HOW MUCH WATER DOES THE SYSTEM USE?

A flow sensor installed in the water line detects water movement through pulse signals. The ESP32 uses these pulses to calculate the cumulative amount of water consumed.

This is a cumulative measurement: it keeps adding up as the system operates, rather than showing the water used at one moment.

HOW IT WORKS

The system continuously measures air temperature, relative humidity and wind velocity under the waiting shed. An ESP32-S3 controller checks these readings every 2 seconds and switches the misting pump automatically. No manual operation is needed for automatic misting.

Automatic misting conditions

Temperature ≥ 31 °C
AND relative humidity < 70%
AND wind velocity between 0 and 4 m/s

If any one condition is not satisfied, the pump remains OFF. These are the project's programmed operating conditions, not universal environmental standards.

Pump cycle

Pump ON
30 seconds
Cooldown
3 minutes

After the cooldown, the environmental conditions are checked again. During cooldown the dashboard can show that conditions are satisfied while the pump is still resting.

How values are obtained

  • Temperature and humidity: the average of the valid readings from three DHT22 sensors.
  • Heat index: calculated from temperature and humidity using the NOAA/NWS method, shown in °C.
  • Wind velocity: measured with an anemometer.
  • Water consumption: a flow sensor produces pulses as water passes through; the ESP32 converts them into cumulative liters.
  • Electrical consumption: measured separately with an electrical submeter.

Data flow

Sensors→ESP32-S3→Wi-Fi→Online database→This website

Readings are uploaded about every 2 seconds. The database summarizes them into 1-minute, hourly, daily and monthly records for the Historical Data page. The original 2-second measurements are also saved on an SD card as the experimental record. This website only reads data; it cannot control the system.

ABOUT THE PROJECT

Design and Development of a Roof-Mounted Misting System to Provide Thermal Comfort in Outdoor Campus Spaces

Institution: Polytechnic University of the Philippines – Institute of Technology (PUP ITECH)
Author: LIBIRAN JR., HENRY MICHAEL S.

Purpose

The project aims to monitor thermal conditions in an outdoor campus space and to provide automated misting that supports thermal comfort, so that the system's operation can be evaluated with real measurements.

Location and context

The system is designed for an outdoor or semi-outdoor waiting shed at PUP ITECH.

Monitoring and automatic misting

Temperature, relative humidity and wind velocity are measured continuously. When the programmed conditions are satisfied, the controller operates the misting pump on a 30-second ON and 3-minute cooldown cycle.

Data collection and resource evaluation

Environmental data are stored locally and online for analysis. Water consumption is measured with a flow sensor, and electrical consumption with a separate electrical submeter, so that the resources needed to operate the system can be examined.

About the numbers on this website

This website only shows actual live and stored measurements received from the system. It does not present performance results or comparisons.
